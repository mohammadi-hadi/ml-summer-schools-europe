A practical guide to ML/AI summer schools in Europe: which ones exist, how to get in, how to fund it, and how to get real value out of the week. Written by someone who has sat in those lecture halls — I attended the Explainable AI Summer School (TU Delft, 2022, on a scholarship), the Oxford Machine Learning Summer School (2023), the Generative Modeling Summer School (Eindhoven, 2024), and a LERU doctoral summer school (2025).

Most lists tell you the schools exist. This one also tells you what the application actually needs, when the deadlines cluster, and what separates people who get a week of content from people who get collaborators.

## The schools

Dates and venues move every year — always confirm on the school's site. "Usually" reflects the typical pattern of recent editions.

| School | Where | Focus | Usually | Site |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| EEML (Eastern European ML Summer School) | rotating, Eastern Europe | modern deep learning | July | [eeml.eu](https://www.eeml.eu/) |
| M2L (Mediterranean ML Summer School) | rotating, Mediterranean | ML and deep learning | September | [m2lschool.org](https://www.m2lschool.org/) |
| LxMLS (Lisbon ML School) | Lisbon, Portugal | ML for NLP | July | [lxmls.it.pt](http://lxmls.it.pt/) |

| OxML (Oxford ML Summer School) | Oxford / hybrid, UK | ML with health and finance tracks | summer, multiple cohorts | [oxfordml.school](https://www.oxfordml.school/) |

| ACAI (Advanced Course on AI, by EurAI) | rotating | thematic AI school | varies | [eurai.org](https://eurai.org/) |

Two schools I attended have no stable long-lived site (the Explainable AI Summer School in Delft and the Generative Modeling Summer School in Eindhoven) — editions of both were announced through university pages; search by name and year.

## When to apply

- Applications for summer editions cluster **January–April**, with decisions April–May. If you are reading this in autumn, set calendar reminders now; several schools close in a two-week window and do not extend.
- Competitive schools (EEML, LxMLS, MLSS editions) regularly report acceptance rates well under half. Apply to two or three, not one.
- Some schools run tracks or cohorts (OxML) — applying early cohorts usually means less competition than the popular mid-summer ones.

## Funding it

- **School scholarships**: most schools waive fees (sometimes travel) for a subset of participants — there is usually a checkbox in the application; tick it and write the one-paragraph justification properly. My XAISS attendance was funded exactly this way.
- **Your graduate school / department**: most European PhD programs have an annual training budget per candidate that summer schools squarely qualify for. Ask before you assume there is nothing.
- **National AI networks and ELLIS units** occasionally fund attendance for members — worth one email.
- Budget realistically: fee (€0–1,500), accommodation (often the larger cost), travel. Early application sometimes unlocks cheaper university housing.

## The application, done well

Selection committees read hundreds of near-identical "I am passionate about AI" statements. What works:

1. **Fit over enthusiasm.** Name the two or three lecturers or topics on this year's program and connect them to what you are working on right now. It proves you read the program.
2. **One concrete goal.** "I want to discuss my annotation-reliability results with people working on human–AI evaluation" beats any paragraph about the future of AI.
3. **Evidence you'll contribute**: a poster, a preprint, an open-source project, even a well-scoped question. Schools select participants who make the school better.
4. If a recommendation letter is optional, treat it as required — and ask your supervisor three weeks early, not three days.

## Getting the value out

- **Bring a poster even if your work is early.** The poster session is where the actual conversations happen; "work in progress" posters start better discussions than finished ones.
- Talk to the TAs and early-career speakers, not only the famous names — they have time, and they review for the venues you submit to.
- Write down one sentence per person you want to follow up with, and send those emails **within a week**, while they remember you.
- The lectures are recorded or slides are shared more often than not; the people are not. Optimize accordingly.
